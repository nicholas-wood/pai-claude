I don't think we should close this out. I think that would be the wrong call for the building and far from doing an adequate job as a working group.

Moving to EV ready is a "when," not an "if." EV-ready infrastructure is already standard in new Victorian builds, adoption keeps climbing, and the cost of fitting a building like ours won't fall over time. 

Whilst I agree the dollar figure is high, I’m not sure we should have been expecting much different given the scope of work we are looking in to. If we break it down across all the lots, it’s quite reasonable given the impact of the work. It works out to about $22 per entitlement unit, so roughly $1,060 for the smallest lot up to around $2,730 for the largest, as a one-off. 

If we weigh the one-off cost against the impact to both our individual unit values, sale prospects and potential rental yields if we continue to defer being EV ready, we will only continue to appeal to less and less of the market as EV adoption continues to grow. Even for the biggest lots, I would suggest that the impact of not being EV ready will far outweigh any one-off cost. Simply saying no will ultimately leave every single OC member worse off.

The $125k is also only indicative. We haven't even got to the point of asking Origin for the firm quote they offered. Rather than walk away, I'd suggest we explore some questions first: 
-    Is there a cheaper alternative, from Origin or a competitor? 
-    Could the resident cost be staged over a few years instead of paid up front? 
-    Can the OC absorb the backbone over time, or could Origin offer an instalment plan? 
-    Is a staged rollout viable, part of the capacity now and the rest as we approach it, to spread the cost further? 

Closing this out without even looking into the options properly is not the purpose of the working group.
